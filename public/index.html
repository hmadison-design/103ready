Sixteen branching scenarios where you are the pilot in command, the information is incomplete, and the airplane does exactly what airplanes do when you get it wrong.

Each one takes fifteen to forty minutes. There are no right-answer buzzers. You make the call, you live with it, and the debrief at the end tells you what it cost.

  • One Eighty

    Marginal VFR across the Allegheny ridges to a reunion dinner, in a Warrior, with 620 hours and no instrument rating. The early 180 is cheap. The late one is not.

  • The Forty-Five

These scenarios are written for pilots with a few hundred hours who already know the regulations and want practice at the part the regulations cannot cover: judgment with incomplete information. The narrator never grades you mid-flight. Every ending has a debrief that explains what happened and why, and the ones that go badly are the ones worth replaying.

Nothing here records who you are. The site counts starts and endings anonymously so we know which scenarios get finished and which ones lose people, and that is all.

For general aviation pilots. Training fiction, not a substitute for PIC judgment.
